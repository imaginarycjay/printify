## 2. Ready-to-Use Text: `CHAPTER 2 — REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE`

### 2.1 Job Scheduling, Multi-Printer Allocation, and Production Queue Management

> In make-to-order (MTO) printing environments, production scheduling directly governs equipment utilization, staff workload balance, and customer turnaround reliability. Unlike repetitive batch manufacturing, commercial print shops process heterogeneous job streams where each order item varies in run length, substrate weight, color duplexing, and post-press finishing (Jebbor et al., 2023). Oktavian et al. (2024) demonstrated that replacing manual whiteboard and logbook queues with a web-based production scheduling system significantly reduces job bottlenecks and prevents lost work tickets in small-to-medium printing enterprises. However, treating an entire customer order as a single indivisible job introduces severe scheduling rigidity when an order combines rapid-turnaround digital prints (such as single-sheet flyers) with multi-day finishing tasks (such as hardbound academic manuscripts). Lee and Lo (2019) emphasized that decomposing composite orders into item-level production tasks allows shop floor schedulers to route individual items across parallel workstations and release finished items early without waiting for long-lead-time components.
>
> Effective print shop scheduling also requires visibility into both machine availability and operator workload across shop boundaries. Sithi et al. (2020) noted that production delays frequently occur when incoming jobs are assigned without accounting for the active queue depth of specific printers or on-duty technicians. Extending this principle to a multi-tenant marketplace environment, surfacing each shop's real-time queue load indicator (*Available*, *Moderate Load*, or *Heavily Loaded*) empowers customers to make informed ordering decisions before submitting time-sensitive jobs. Furthermore, bridging the digital queue and physical press execution requires direct integration between the management platform and the workstation's local print spooler. When operators download customer files to desktop folders and trigger print commands externally, the scheduling system loses visibility over when printing actually commences and how many physical sheets are expended—a gap resolved by initiating print jobs directly from the production queue interface through the operating system's printer driver (Jebbor et al., 2023; Oktavian et al., 2024).

### 2.2 Multi-Tenant Order Management, Prepress Verification, Billing, and Storage Retention

> Digital transformation in the printing industry increasingly favors cloud-based, multi-tenant Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) architectures that enable multiple independent printing shops to operate isolated storefronts, catalogs, and production queues on a shared infrastructure. Within each tenant workspace, automated order intake must translate variable customer parameters—such as paper gsm, dimensions, page count, color coverage, and binding style—into deterministic price quotes (Oktavian et al., 2024). Before a custom print job enters active press scheduling, prepress validation is essential to verify document resolution, bleed margins, and file integrity. Once production staff approve an uploaded artwork file, locking the item's technical specifications prevents uncoordinated mid-production changes—such as switching from 200gsm to 300gsm cardstock—unless a formal specification revision request pauses the item and recalculates both material reservations and required downpayment adjustments.
>
> Financial risk mitigation in custom printing relies heavily on upfront payment gating. Because customized print runs cannot be restocked or resold if a customer abandons an order, enforcing a mandatory 50% downpayment prior to press queueing protects shop working capital (Susila et al., 2025). When integrating digital e-wallet gateways (such as GCash or Maya), distributed network interruptions can occasionally sever webhook callbacks after funds have been deducted from the customer's account; consequently, resilient transaction systems combine idempotent webhook handlers with scheduled payment-status polling and manual reference-number verification fallbacks. Equally important to long-term SaaS sustainability is automated file storage hygiene. High-resolution print-ready PDF, TIFF, and PNG files rapidly consume server disk capacity if retained indefinitely after job completion. Implementing an automated file retention policy that purges heavy artwork binaries after a configurable post-completion window—while preserving lightweight order metadata and official billing records—ensures sustainable multi-tenant server operation.

### 2.3 Inventory Control, Atomic BOM Reservation, Spoilage Tracking, and Standardized Reporting

> Raw material inventory in commercial printing is tightly coupled to order specifications through a Bill of Materials (BOM) structure, where a single print item consumes proportional quantities of paper sheets, ink or toner, laminate film, and binding supplies (Susila et al., 2025). Traditional inventory systems that deduct stock only at final checkout or manual end-of-day tallies fail to prevent over-allocation when multiple customers simultaneously order from a limited paper batch. Applying pessimistic database row-locking (`SELECT ... FOR UPDATE`) and atomic stock reservation during order confirmation ensures that concurrent requests for limited substrates are serialized at the database layer, immediately alerting subsequent users if available stock falls below their requested quantity. Moreover, physical print production inevitably incurs material waste during press alignment or quality-assurance (QA) failures. Separating customer billing records from warehouse stock ledger movements (`rework_spoilage`) allows production staff to record wasted sheets from QA reruns accurately without double-charging the customer.
>
> To prevent unexpected stockouts of fast-moving paper grades and consumables, automated Reorder Point (ROP) monitoring continuously evaluates available stock against minimum safety thresholds (Abu Zwaida et al., 2021; Teerasoponpong & Sopadang, 2022). When inventory reaches its configured reorder point, automated in-app and email alerts notify the shop owner to initiate timely restocking. Beyond real-time alerts, shop owners require standardized, printable, and downloadable operational reports to support physical audits and financial reconciliation (Susila et al., 2025). Generating structured PDF and CSV reports for **Inventory and Stock Movements**, **Verified Sales and Revenue**, and **Live Schedule and Job Status** (*identifying which jobs are currently printing, which are already printed, and which are ready for release*) provides tenant administrators with complete audit trails across both production and warehouse operations.

### 2.4 Functional Testing and Quality Assurance

> Verifying a multi-tenant transaction and scheduling system requires a structured software testing methodology that evaluates whether implemented workflows conform to their specified functional requirements. According to the international software testing standard **ISO/IEC/IEEE 29119-1:2022**, functional testing is a black-box verification approach that assesses system behavior against documented requirements by supplying defined inputs and comparing observed outputs against expected acceptance criteria. Rather than relying on subjective respondent impressions, requirements-based testing provides objective, repeatable evidence of software correctness across distinct user roles, data validation boundaries, and concurrent transaction rules (ISO/IEC/IEEE, 2022).
>
> As defined in **ISO/IEC/IEEE 29119-2:2021** (*Test Processes*) and **ISO/IEC/IEEE 29119-3:2021** (*Test Documentation*), a comprehensive verification cycle combines three complementary testing stages: **Smoke Testing**, **Functional Testing**, and **Regression Testing**. **Smoke Testing** serves as the initial build-verification gate, confirming that core application routes, role-based authentication guards, tenant workspace isolation, and database connections load and operate without critical failures before detailed module testing begins. **Functional Testing** then systematically exercises every functional requirement—including normal execution paths, boundary conditions, validation errors, and role-permission restrictions—using structured test case specifications (`Test Case ID`, `Module/Feature`, `Objective`, `Preconditions`, `Test Steps`, `Expected Result`, `Actual Result`, and `Status`: *Passed*, *Failed*, or *Needs Revision*) (ISO/IEC/IEEE, 2021b). Finally, whenever a defect is identified and corrected during development, **Regression Testing** re-executes the previously failed test case alongside adjacent workflow dependencies to ensure that code modifications resolve the defect without introducing unintended side effects in previously verified modules (ISO/IEC/IEEE, 2021a).

## 3. Ready-to-Use Text: `RESEARCH GAP`

> A synthesis of existing literature and commercial platforms reveals a clear operational gap for independent and campus-adjacent printing shops. While enterprise-grade ERP and Web-to-Print suites exist for large industrial presses, their licensing costs and complex configurations are prohibitive for small-to-medium printing shops, which instead rely on disconnected spreadsheets, messaging apps, and manual job tickets (Oktavian et al., 2024; Susila et al., 2025). Conversely, lightweight local ordering systems surveyed in prior studies typically operate as single-shop installations that lack multi-tenant SaaS workspace isolation, provide customers with no visibility into whether a shop is already heavily loaded with pending orders, and track orders as monolithic tickets rather than scheduling individual order items across multiple printers and staff members (Jebbor et al., 2023). Furthermore, existing local prototypes rarely integrate direct system-to-printer driver spooling to capture exact sheet consumption, decoupled quality-assurance spoilage accounting, pessimistic database row-locking for concurrent stock reservations, automated post-completion print file cleanup, and standardized printable reports covering live job status, stock movements, and sales.
>
> **PressQueue** addresses this gap by uniting multi-tenant marketplace load transparency, multi-printer and multi-staff item-level job scheduling, direct printer-driver spooling, prepress specification locking with 50% downpayment verification, atomic BOM inventory control with ROP alerting, automated file retention cleanup, and standardized printable/downloadable reporting within a single web platform. The scope of this capstone project is intentionally focused on software design, implementation, requirements-based smoke, functional, and regression testing aligned with ISO/IEC/IEEE 29119, and production deployment-readiness planning; it does not include a separate external human-respondent usability or post-deployment longitudinal survey.
