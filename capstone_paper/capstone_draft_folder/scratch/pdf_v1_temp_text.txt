 INTRODUCTION 
 The commercial printing and binding sector operates within a fast - paced 
 environment   catering to   diverse   customer demands,   ranging   from   routine 
 document   printing   and   seasonal   thesis   binding   to   commercial   marketing 
 collaterals.   As   academic   institutions   and   l ocal   commercial   enterprises 
 continuously   generate   demand,   printing   service   providers   face   persistent 
 hurdles in sustaining operational efficiency and consistent turnaround times 
 (Saragih et al., 2026; Susila et al., 2025). Despite handling complex custom j ob 
 specifications, many local printing establishments continue to rely on manual, 
 paper - based workflows or fragmented spreadsheet tracking systems (Apriadi & 
 Dewi, 2026; Tanaman et al., 2023). These unintegrated setups create severe 
 operational bottlenecks , especially during peak academic semester deadlines 
 when order volumes surge rapidly (Jebbor et al., 2021; Kurniawan et al., 2025). 
 Without   a   synchronized   management   framework,   delegating   tasks   across 
 workshop   machinery   and   production   personnel   becomes   di sorganized 
 (Smagowicz et al., 2024), while the absence of real - time material monitoring 
 frequently results in unexpected stockouts of essential supplies like paper 
 stocks, toner, and binding covers, leading to costly delays and compromised 
 customer satisfa ction (Abu Zwaida et al., 2021; Albayrak Ünal et al., 2023). 
 To   resolve   these   operational   limitations,   conventional   software 
 approaches   prove   inadequate   because   they   are   often   rigidly   hardcoded, 
 preventing shop owners from introducing new service variations or modifying 
=====PAGE 15=====
2 
 dynamic pricing rules without modifying underlying application source code 
 (Joshi et al., 2026; Saragih et al., 2026). In response, this capstone project 
 develops   the   Integrated   Dynamic   Order,   Job   Scheduling,   and   Inventory 
 Management System for Printing Se rvices with Automated Replenishment 
 (Printify).   The   platform   introduces   a   modular   web   architecture   enabling 
 administrators to configure service catalogs, page rates, and linked Bill of 
 Materials   (BOM)   recipes   dynamically   through   an   intuitive   interface.   To 
 streamline   shop   floor   productivity ,   an   integrated   job   scheduling   console 
 organizes active orders into a visual production queue, systematically allocating 
 machinery and labor to maintain delivery schedules (Smagowicz et al., 2024; 
 Zonta et al., 2022). Furthermore, instead of relying on lon g - term predictive 
 models requiring extensive historical datasets, the system calculates the real - 
 time consumption burn rate of raw materials directly from active sales volume, 
 automatically triggering restocking alerts when inventory levels reach critical 
 reorder thresholds (Jebbor et al., 2021; Ugbebor et al., 2024). Uniting dynamic 
 order intake, visual floor tracking, and automated material replenishment within 
 a centralized web platform modernizes print shop operations, ensuring punctual 
 fulfillment, bal anced labor allocation, and data - driven inventory control (Apriadi 
 & Dewi, 2026; Bhaktha et al., 2023).

 Significance of the Project 
 The realization of this project will profoundly benefit various stakeholders 
 within the printing and binding industry by transforming disjointed manual 
 processes   into   a   streamlined,   automated   workflow.   Primarily,   Business 
 Owners and Management   will gain a dynamic and highly adaptable platform 
 that allows them to effortlessly configure, add, or modify printing services and 
 pricing   structures   without   needing   complex   source   code   alterations. 
 Furthermore, the automated inventory replenishment modul e will eliminate the 
 guesswork in restocking, calculating real - time material burn rates to prevent 
 costly   stockouts   and   maximize   potential   revenue,   especially   during   peak 
 seasons. 
 For   Employees and Production Staff , the system will significantly 
 reduce the chaos associated with sudden influxes of orders. The dedicated job 
 scheduling module will provide a clear, visual queue of assigned tasks and 
 deadlines, preventing overlapping duties and allowing staff to focus ef ficiently 
 on production rather than manual coordination. Consequently,   Customers and 
 Clients   will experience highly reliable service. With optimized order tracking, 
 systematic job delegation, and guaranteed raw material availability, clients will 
 benefit from faster turnaround times, strict adherence to deadlines, and an 
 overall enhanced customer   experience. 
 Finally, this study will serve as a substantial foundation for   Future 
 Researchers and Developers . The integration of a dynamic, configurable

 Statement of the Problem 
 This study addresses the overarching problem of how to design and 
 develop   an   Integrated   Dynamic   Order,   Job   Scheduling,   and   Inventory 
 Management System for Printing Services with Automated Replenishment to 
 effectively resolve the manual operational bottlene cks experienced by printing 
 establishments. Currently, the reliance on disjointed, traditional processes 
 creates significant frustrations for all primary stakeholders. For customers, the 
 absence of real - time order tracking leads to the inconvenience of rep eatedly 
 visiting or contacting the establishment just to check the status of their print jobs, 
 often resulting in dissatisfaction when orders are unexpectedly delayed. For 
 production staff and employees, depending on manual, paper - based queuing 
 systems cre ates a chaotic workflow where physical job tickets are easily 
 misplaced, miscommunicated, or improperly prioritized, leading to overlapping 
 duties   and   unfulfilled   deadlines.   Meanwhile,   business   owners   and 
 administrators are hindered by rigid software that   cannot adapt to new services, 
=====PAGE 18=====
5 
 and they are forced to rely on pure guesswork and intuition to manage inventory, 
 which inevitably leads to unexpected material stockouts and lost revenue during 
 peak seasons. 
 To resolve these interconnected operational challenges, the project must 
 explicitly address how to formulate a dynamic order management architecture 
 that allows administrators to configure services, pricing tariffs, and consumable 
 requirements   on   the   fly   w ithout   modifying   application   source   cod e,   while 
 enabling customers to configure print specifications, obtain instant quotations, 
 and submit orders online . Furthermore, it must establish how to engineer an 
 interactive production floor scheduling module that digitizes workshop queues, 
 visually tracks order progression across five defined stages, and systematically 
 delegates   machinery   and   labor.   Concurrently,   it   must   determine   how   to 
 implement an automated inventory replenishment module that computes daily 
 material burn rates , records material spoilage,   and triggers timely replenishment 
 alerts at dynamic reorder points. Finally, the project must answer how to 
 integrate these targeted modules into a single, cohesive web - based platform 
 governed by role - based access control , and how to conduct rigorous software 
 functional testing and system verification using a structured Test Plan to 
 empirically validate that all functional requirements execute accurately on a 
 pass - or - fail basis.

 Scope and Limitations of the Study 
 The scope of this project is strictly bounded to the order configuration, 
 job scheduling, and inventory replenishment of printing services. The 
 system is not designed to function as a full - scale Enterprise Resource 
 Planning (ERP) software; thus, it complet ely excludes comprehensive 
 financial   accounting,   third - party   logistics   or   courier   integration,   and 
 Human Resource modules such as payroll and biometric employee 
 attendance.   The sales and consumption reports generated by the system 
 are limited to operational summaries derived from recorded orders, 
 material usage, and spoilage; they do not constitute formal bookkeeping, 
 tax   computation,   or   audited   financial   statements.   Furthermore,   the 
 system relies on manual payment verificat ion (e.g., uploading of GCash 
 receipts) and does not integrate with automated bank APIs or live 
 payment gateways. Lastly, the software is purely web - based and does 
 not feature Internet of Things (IoT) capabilities; it will not directly interface 
 with physi cal printing or binding machines to automatically read hardware 
 statuses or ink levels.

 to ensure smooth transactions. This includes customer profiles and contact 
 details, transaction histories, and uploaded print files. On the administrative 
 side, the database will handle   the dynamic service configurations (custom 
 pricing and variations), real - time job order statuses, and automated inventory 
 records, specifically the current stock levels, material burn rates, and reorder 
 point thresholds.

 Operational Definition of Terms 
 To ensure clarity and establish a shared understanding of the concepts 
 discussed throughout this study, the following terms are operationally defined. 
 These definitions reflect how each technical and business - specific term is 
 explicitly   utilized   within   the   context   of   the   developed   system's   dynamic 
=====PAGE 25=====
12 
 architecture, modules, and workflows,   rather than   their general dictionary 
 meanings: 
 Administrator   -   Refers to the business owner or authorized management 
 personnel   who   has   full   access   to   the   system.   They   are   responsible   for 
 dynamically   configuring   printing   services,   managing   pricing   variables, 
 monitoring the inventory burn rate, and verifying customer p ayments. 
 Automated   Replenishment   -   Refers   to   the   specific   system module   that 
 eliminates   manual   stock   guesswork   by   calculating   real - time   material 
 consumption and automatically triggering alerts when supplies reach a critical 
 level. 
 Burn Rate   -   In this study, it refers to the calculated velocity or speed at which 
 specific raw printing materials (e.g., paper, ink, binding covers) are consumed 
 based on active, short - term sales volume. 
 Customer   -   Refers to the clients or end - users who utilize the customer - facing 
 web   portal   to   browse   offered   printing   services,   submit   specific   order 
 requirements, upload files, and visually track the real - time status of their print 
 jobs. 
 Dynamic Configuration   -   Refers to the core architectural feature of the system 
 that allows administrators to seamlessly create, add, or modify printing services 
 and pricing structures through the user interface without requiring continuous 
 modifications to the backend source code .

13 
 Inventory Management   -   Refers to the digital tracking system used to monitor 
 the availability, quantity, and depletion of raw materials required for production, 
 which automatically updates as production staff complete their assigned tasks. 
 Job Scheduling   -   Refers to the digital production queue that replaces physical 
 job tickets. It visually tracks the progress of each order and systematically 
 allocates labor and machinery to ensure fulfillment deadlines are met. 
 Printing Services   -   Refers to the customizable products and services offered

 Production Staff   -   Refers to the shop employees who utilize the job scheduling 
 module to view their assigned tasks, update the production progress of specific 
 orders, and initiate the digital deduction of consumed raw materials. 
 Reorder Point   -   Refers to the specific, data - driven stock level threshold 
 calculated   by   the   system   which,   when   reached,   immediately   notifies   the 
 administrator that new raw materials must be purchased from suppliers.

 Dynamic Order 
 Apriadi and Dewi (2026) highlighted that many small and medium - sized 
 printing businesses still rely on manual or semi - digital processes, resulting in 
 miscommunication, data inconsistencies, and inefficient workflow management. 
 To address these challenges,   the researchers developed a web - based printing 
 service   system   that   integrates   order   management,   inventory   control,   and 
 production workflow into a unified platform. The findings demonstrated that an 
 integrated   information   system   can   reduce   operational   error s,   improve 
 communication   between   departments,   and   enhance   overall   operational 
 efficiency. Similarly, Susila et al. (2025) identified limitations in order recording, 
 cost estimation, and production monitoring, where manual processes can cause 
 delays and err ors. Their study developed a web - based system with online 
 ordering, automated cost estimation, job tracking, and real - time updates, which 
 improved production efficiency and customer satisfaction. Saragih et al. (2026) 
 likewise emphasized the increasing dem and for efficiency and accuracy in 
 service industries and noted that many organizations, particularly small and 
 medium - sized enterprises such as printing services, still rely on manual or semi - 
 digital   processes.   Their   study   addressed   operational   inefficien cies,   data 
 inconsistencies,   and   communication   gaps   through   the   design   and 
 implementation of a web - based information system for digital printing services.

 at an Indonesian packaging manufacturer resulted in production inaccuracies,

 storage, and communication, with a cloud - based data layer enabling real - time 
 connectivity   between users and print shops. The findings showed that the 
 system was more efficient and easier to use than traditional print management

 METHODOLOGY 
 This   chapter   outlines   the   research   procedures   and   engineering 
 techniques employed in designing, constructing, and evaluating the Integrated 
 Dynamic   Order,   Job   Scheduling,   and   Inventory   Management   System   for 
 Printing Services with Automated Replenishment. I t details the research design, 
 operational characterization of participants, project materials, empirical data 
 collection techniques, statistical analysis procedures, system validity measures, 
 and ethical safeguards. Furthermore, it presents the architectu ral requirements 
 and software modeling diagrams that guided the systematic implementation and 
 quality assessment of the platform. 
 Project Design 
 This study employs a Developmental Research Design, a methodology 
 centered on the systematic analysis, design, development, implementation, and 
 evaluation of an instructional or technological solution to address operational 
 problems. This research design   is suited for this study because the primary 
 objective is to engineer a functional, integrated web - based information system 
 that   unites   dynamic   order   management,   floor   job   scheduling,   inventory 
 management, and automated replenishment. 
 Through   the   developmental   framework,   the   project   developers 
 systematically   establish   operational   requirements   from   real - world   printing 
=====PAGE 40=====
27 
 workflows, translate these requirements into modular software components, 
 conduct   rigorous   verification,   and   evaluate   the   final   system   through   a 
 comprehensive Software Quality Assurance (SQA) and Functional Testing 
 protocol. This ensures that the resulting   software artifact is practically grounded, 
 technically sound, and empirically validated against all specified functional 
 requirements on a pass - or - fail basis.

 Data Analysis Procedure 
 1.   System Testing and Functional Verification Plan 
 To rigorously evaluate the software without introducing subjective 
 evaluation bias, the project developers implemented a formal Software 
 Quality Assurance (SQA) and Functional Verification protocol. Rather 
 than relying on perceptual survey ratings, system   verification is governed 
=====PAGE 43=====

 by empirical, binary evaluation criteria (Pass or Fail) modeled after IEEE 
 829 software test documentation standards. 
 2.   Multi - tier testing Strategy 
 The system evaluation follows a two - tier verification architecture: 
 Tier   1:   Developer   Functional   and   Automated   System   Testing 
 (Primary   Verification):   The   project   developers   execute   discrete 
 functional test cases covering normal, boundary, and exceptional input 
 conditions across all 1 9   Functional Requirements (FR - 01 to FR - 1 9 ). In 
 addition,   core   computational   routines — including   dynamic   pricing 
 algorithms, duplex paper calculations, automated Bill of Materials (BOM) 
 inventory   deductions,   and   rolling   Reorder   Point   (ROP)   alerts — are 
 continuously verified using automated unit and f eature test suites built on 
 the Pest/PHPUnit testing framework. 
 Tier 2: Stakeholder Operational Walkthrough (User Acceptance 
 Testing):   Printing shop   owners and production personnel execute end - 
 to - end   operational   scenarios   simulating   daily   commercial   activities, 
 including customer counter intake, job ticket delegation across Kanban 
 columns, material spoilage adjustments, and financial report exports. 
 Th e walkthrough validates task completion success, business workflow 
 alignment, and operational ergonomics.

 user telemetry.

 Project Design 
 The project design translates the operational requirements and technical 
 constraints into actionable architectural blueprints. This stage bridges business 
 rules with software implementation. Structurally, it defines the normalized data 
 models and relationa l constraints that synchronize incoming print orders with 
 automated Bill of Materials (BOM) inventory deductions. Behaviorally, it outlines 
 how business owners, production operators, and customers interact across core 
 functional modules, tracking jobs as t hey advance through workshop queues 
 from intake to final handoff. The succeeding discussions present the logical 
 database schema through an Entity Relationship Diagram (ERD), followed by

 Entity Relationship Diagram 
 This Figure illustrates the Entity Relationship Diagram (ERD) of the 
 system, detailing the logical entities, primary keys, and foreign key constraints 
 across the normalized database tables. At the architectural center is the   Users 
 table (user_id), governing authentication and role - based access control (RBAC) 
 across business owners, workshop staff, and customers. A single registered 
 business owner manages an enterprise instance in the   PrintShops   table

 (print_shop_id)   through   a   strict   one - to - one   relationship   (owner_user_id), 
 anchoring all tenant - level operations. Each print shop maintains its dynamic 
 service catalog through the   ShopServices   table (service_id, referenced via 
 print_shop_id 1:N). Rather than relying on separate tables for each print 
 offering, this unified entity encapsulates production lead times, daily shop floor 
 capacity quotas, and custom pricing rules within a structured se ttings JSON 
 attribute, allowing owners to update service parameters without altering the 
 underlying database schema. 
 Customer transactions and workshop scheduling converge in the   Orders 
 table (order_id, referenced from print_shop_id 1:N). This entity links to   Users 
 through two distinct foreign keys: customer_user_id for order placement and 
 assigned_staff_user_id for production delegation. It tracks human - readable 
 tracking codes, verified payment receipts, and the active workstation state 
 across a five - stage Kanban p ipeline ( Queue ,   Printing ,   Finishing/Assembly , 
 Quality Check , and   Ready for Pickup ). Each order contains individual line items 
 captured in the   OrderItems   table (order_item_id, referenced via order_id 1:N).

 item   design   with   a   specifications   JSON   attribute.   This   structure   flexibly 
 accommodates varying parameters — such as page counts,   paper dimensions, 
 binding finishes, or garment sizes — while preserving strict relational integrity 
 with the parent transaction.
